Checkpoint

Nothing new connects without you

Turn Checkpoint on and an unmatched connection doesn't quietly take a default. It waits.

  • The connection holds

    A window comes up with the app that asked, where it wants to go, and your options. The connection stays pending until you answer.

  • Four ways out

    Allow it, block it, hand it to the FINAL rule, or pick any policy from the dropdown — per connection.

  • Answer once

    Flip “remember” and your decision is written as a process rule you can find, edit, reorder or disable later.

  • Never blocking

    Walk away and the FINAL rule applies when the timeout runs out. Nothing hangs waiting on you.

Policy

Routing on your terms

Three outbound modes, eight protocols, and policies that nest inside each other. Build the exact topology you want, then let a balancer keep it healthy.

httpsocks shadowsockstrojan vmessvless hysteria2tuic
  • Policies inside policies

    A policy member can be a proxy or another policy. Manual picks by hand, Auto picks the lowest latency.

  • Six balancing strategies

    Round Robin, Weighted Round Robin, Least Connections, Consistent Hashing, Sticky Sessions and Failover.

  • Proxy chains

    Send traffic through several hops in order when one is not enough to get out.

Rules

A routing table, not a config file

Rules are evaluated top to bottom and the first match wins. Drag to reorder, toggle without deleting, and leave yourself a comment on every line. The last row is always FINAL — the fate of everything that matched nothing.

DNS

DNS, all the way down

The layer most tools hide behind one input field. Zeytun opens all of it — resolvers, transports, per-domain routing, local overrides and a lookup tool that shows its work.

DoHDoT QUICTCPUDP
  • FakeIP engine

    Route by domain instead of resolved address, and stop DNS from leaking around your rules. Flush the core and FakeIP caches in one click.

  • Remote and local, split

    External names resolve through the proxy, internal names stay on your network — over DoH, DoT, QUIC, TCP or UDP.

  • DNS rules

    An ordered rule list for queries, matched the same way traffic is. Send a domain to your router, to a resolver of your choice, or refuse to answer it at all.

  • Lookup that explains itself

    Type a domain and see which server answered, over which transport, with what records, and how long it took.

Architecture

The engine underneath

What the interface can promise depends entirely on what runs below it. Change a rule mid-download and the download keeps going — that is a property of the core, not of the window.

  • Rules that apply live

    Add, edit or reorder a rule and it is in effect immediately. Nothing is torn down to make the change — active downloads, streams and calls keep running.

  • TUN mode

    Capture traffic at the interface, including the apps that quietly ignore a system proxy.

  • A core we ship ourselves

    Not a wrapper around whatever is installed on your machine. The core ships with the app, and we patch and update it on our own schedule.

Roadmap

Where this is going

Three phases, in order. Each one is a promise rather than a feature list, and none of them carry a date we can't keep.

Now Phase 01

Something you can trust on macOS

  • Right under pressure: process attribution that holds up, DNS that never slips past your rules, a core that recovers on its own
  • Checkpoint, finished — every unmatched connection, every time
  • A stable v1 with signed updates, and a setup that survives them

Phase 02

The same setup on every machine

  • Windows and Linux, each with real TUN capture and process routing — not a cut-down build
  • Profiles that travel with you between your own machines
  • One licence, every platform it runs on

Phase 03

More than a proxy

  • Take an app off the network entirely, instead of only deciding where it goes
  • Connection history you can search back through, not just watch go by
  • A core fully in our hands, and near-zero cost while you are idle
